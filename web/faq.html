How to start a dub, what happens while it runs, and how to tell when an English master is ready to release.

What is Dubline?

Dubline turns a local film or audio programme into an English-language dub. It finds the dialogue, separates it from music and effects, works out who is speaking, translates and performs each line, fits those lines to the original timing, then adds a new English track to an MKV.

It is designed for an unattended full-film run. If a line or delivery check cannot be resolved automatically, the film is still produced when possible and is clearly marked Needs review.

01

GETTING STARTED

Sources, subtitles and the first run

Which files can I use?

You can use common consumer and professional video containers, including MKV, MP4, MOV, AVI, WebM, MPEG, WMV, MXF, VOB, MTS/M2TS and 3GP. Audio-only programmes can be WAV, FLAC, MP3, M4A, AAC, OGG, Opus, WMA or AIFF.

Dubline needs a readable audio programme. It can process an entire source or a precise start/end section.

Do I need to provide subtitles?

No. Dubline checks usable sidecar and embedded subtitles, then falls back to speech recognition when needed. Supported sidecars are SRT, ASS, SSA, VTT and VobSub SUB/IDX pairs.

For one uploaded film, add the subtitle in the same drop. For several films, give each sidecar the same base filename as its film. When you use a local path, Dubline also checks for same-name sidecars beside the source.

Should I drop the film in or use its local path?

Both start the same dubbing process. Drag-and-drop makes a restart-safe copy in the job workspace. For a feature film or a large master, the local-path option avoids that second source copy and is usually the better choice.

Intermediate stems and takes still require substantial working space. If space is tight, the operator can place the Dubline work folder on a larger SSD.

Can I dub only part of a film?

Yes. Enter the section start, end, or both as MM:SS or HH:MM:SS. Leave both blank for the whole film. This is useful for a test scene.

Even for a section, Dubline can scan the whole original film for recurring-character evidence. That wider context helps it distinguish voices more reliably.

Why must I confirm voice rights?

Dubline recreates voice characteristics from the source. You must have the necessary rights and permission to dub the media and reproduce those voices before a job can start.

That confirmation does not grant rights or replace legal advice. Commercial users should also review the licences for the source media and the bundled speech models.

02

DURING A DUB

Automatic decisions and long-running work

What happens after I press “Dub”?

Dubline inspects the media, chooses dialogue evidence, separates speech from music and effects, maps speakers and timing, translates scene by scene, generates and fits English performances, rebuilds the mix, adds the English track, and checks the delivered file.

The progress view groups that work into Inspect, Separate, Understand, Voice, Timeline and Video.

How does it choose the right soundtrack and subtitle track?

It favours full programme tracks and avoids labels such as commentary, audio description, director, isolated score, music-only and karaoke. It also avoids forced/signs-only or commentary subtitles when a full dialogue track is available.

If more than one track is genuinely plausible, the job pauses and asks you to choose. Pick the full dialogue programme—not a commentary or accessibility mix.

How long will a film take?

There is no dependable universal multiple of the film runtime: scene complexity, line count, retries, source quality and this PC’s GPU all matter. The first completed film establishes a local baseline; later jobs show an estimate based on completed work on this machine.

Only one GPU-heavy job runs at a time, so additional films wait in the queue.

Can I pause or recover a job?

Yes. Pause asks the active stage to stop at a safe checkpoint; Resume continues from committed work. If processing stops with an error, use Resume from checkpoint. Dubline keeps cue sheets, references, completed takes and reusable stems so a restart does not intentionally begin from zero.

A queued job pauses immediately. An active model may need a short moment to reach its next safe control point.

What does Cancel do?

Cancel stops future processing and safely terminates active work. Intermediate files remain in the project workspace so the state is not silently destroyed. Removing a project and its workspace is a separate operation; the current end-user screen does not expose a delete button.

03

REVIEW & QUALITY

What “passed” means—and what it does not

What does “Passed” mean?

It means every automatic line check and delivery check produced acceptable evidence: dialogue wording, speaker similarity, timing, performance measures, clipping, leakage, loudness, stream preservation and final-file structure.

It is a strong technical gate, not a promise of creative perfection. For a public or commercial release, a human should still watch and listen to the programme.

What does “Needs review” mean?

Dubline produced a playable dub, but one or more lines or delivery checks remain unresolved. Open the QC report and use the stated reasons rather than treating the warning as a generic failure.

Common causes include uncertain words or speakers, a translation concern, a timing/performance mismatch, overlapping voices, dialogue leakage, or a final loudness/stream-preservation problem.

Can I correct a line without restarting the film?

Yes. A flagged line can be corrected and regenerated on its own. You can revise the source transcript, English line, character name and timing. In the pre-voice approval pause, you can also split or merge cues before continuing.

Changing a character name renames that recognised voice across the project. Edited audio and downstream mix/QC files are rebuilt so an old take cannot slip back into delivery.

How do I review the translation before voices are generated?

Before starting, tick Hold before voicing in the Review row. Dubline completes dialogue analysis and translation, then waits before the lengthy voice stage. Correct any line, then choose Continue to voicing.

How does the pronunciation glossary work?

Add one replacement per line in the form term = spoken form, for example Sato = Sah-toh. The right-hand text is sent to speech generation while the English subtitle wording remains unchanged.

Use it for names, acronyms and unusual terms. It is a literal project-wide replacement, so choose entries that will not accidentally match ordinary words.

04

FILES & DELIVERY

Masters, tracks and useful handoffs

What is in the downloaded MKV?

The MKV keeps the original picture, original streams, chapters and container metadata, then adds an English-labelled FLAC delivery track. The new dub is non-default, so the source’s existing default-track choices are not silently changed.

FLAC describes the delivery codec. It does not mean AI-generated speech is mathematically identical to a studio recording or free of synthesis artefacts.

What else can I export?

Completed projects expose an English SRT, cue CSV, 24 fps CMX-style EDL, a ZIP of per-line audio, the English mix FLAC and the dialogue-only FLAC. The workspace also retains per-character stems and detailed cue state for production handoff.

Does it create surround sound or Atmos?

With a genuine supplied M&E/DME bed, Dubline can preserve or build a stereo, 5.1 or 7.1 English bed as the source allows. Without one, the generated English delivery is explicitly stereo.

It preserves original Atmos and other multichannel source tracks, but it does not author or reconstruct Atmos objects.

Which loudness setting does the end-user screen use?

The simple start screen is fixed to the cinema delivery policy: dialogue-gated −27 LUFS with a −2 dBTP true-peak ceiling. Other mastering policies exist in the underlying production API, but they are not selectable on this end-user start screen.

05

LIMITS & PRIVACY

Clear boundaries before you commit

Does my film or voice leave this computer?

No hosted inference service is used. After the one-time setup has downloaded the software and models, analysis, translation, speech generation, mixing and QC run locally.

Project files remain in Dubline’s local work folder until they are removed. Setup itself needs internet access for downloads, and the optional pyannote model requires its publisher’s agreement and access token.

Can it dub into a language other than English?

Not from this end-user interface. It automatically detects the source language and produces English dialogue. The underlying components understand several languages, but this product flow, labels, quality policy and deliverable are fixed to English.

Can I give it a URL, streaming title, disc or ISO?

No. Dubline accepts local files that FFmpeg can read. It does not download network URLs, bypass streaming or DRM protections, decrypt DVD/Blu-ray discs, or author an ISO. Prepare a lawful, readable local media file first.

Will every voice be an exact clone?

No. Voice matching is confidence-scored synthesis, not a guarantee of biometric identity. Clean recurring speakers get the strongest reference evidence; short, masked, off-screen or uncertain voices may be less exact and are flagged when evidence is weak.

Laughter, crying, breaths, screams, grunts, singing and crowd walla are generally retained from the source performance rather than recreated as English speech.

Does it replace faces or lip-sync the whole film?

No. Normal synchronization is audio-side timing. Optional MuseTalk finishing is disabled by default and, when an operator enables it, is limited to a few clear single-face shots with strong speaker evidence and a remaining visible sync problem.

It deliberately refuses risky material such as HDR/Dolby Vision, 10/12-bit or multi-video-stream sources, and skips group shots, distant faces and unreliable profiles. It is not full-film generative face replacement.

WHAT THIS IS NOT

A careful assistant, not a substitute for a final human listen.

Dubline is built to fail visibly when evidence is weak. A green result means the automated checks passed; a yellow result means the dub exists but needs attention. Neither status transfers rights, guarantees a perfect actor match, or replaces a full listen before release.
